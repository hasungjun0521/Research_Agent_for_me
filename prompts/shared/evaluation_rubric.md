# Research Progress Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate project readiness and decide the next step.

## 1. Clarity of Question

- Strong: The research question is specific, scoped, and testable.
- Adequate: The topic is clear but scope or testability needs refinement.
- Weak: The question is broad, vague, or mostly a topic label.

## 2. Strength of Motivation

- Strong: The problem matters to a defined audience and evidence supports the need.
- Adequate: The importance is plausible but needs stronger support.
- Weak: The motivation relies on generic importance or assumed usefulness.

## 3. Literature Grounding

- Strong: Prior work is mapped, limitations are fair, and gaps are evidence-backed.
- Adequate: Key papers are present but relationships or gaps need sharpening.
- Weak: Novelty claims are unsupported or major baselines are missing.

## 4. Experiment Validity

- Strong: Hypotheses, datasets, baselines, metrics, and ablations align.
- Adequate: Experiments are runnable but some comparisons or diagnostics are weak.
- Weak: Experiments do not test the stated claims.

## 5. Evidence Strength

- Strong: Results are complete, checked, compared to baselines, and caveated.
- Adequate: Results suggest a direction but need reruns, diagnostics, or stronger analysis.
- Weak: Results are incomplete, suspicious, or disconnected from claims.

## 6. Writing Quality

- Strong: Draft is clear, structured, citation-aware, and evidence-aligned.
- Adequate: Draft communicates the idea but overclaims or lacks structure in places.
- Weak: Draft is narrative-first and evidence is missing or unclear.

## 7. Reviewer Risk

- Strong: Likely objections are anticipated and addressed.
- Adequate: Main objections are known but not fully resolved.
- Weak: The project is vulnerable to obvious novelty, baseline, validity, or clarity attacks.

# Academic Style Guide

## General Style

- Be precise.
- Be modest.
- Be evidence-driven.
- Prefer concrete verbs and nouns.
- Avoid hype.
- Avoid vague novelty claims such as "new paradigm" or "first ever" unless the literature review supports them.

## Claim Style

Use claims that state scope:

- Prefer: "On the evaluated datasets, the method improves X relative to Y under Z conditions."
- Avoid: "The method solves X."

Use uncertainty when evidence is limited:

- "These results suggest..."
- "The current evidence does not establish..."
- "A plausible explanation is..."

## Citation Style

- Do not invent citations.
- Cite specific papers for specific claims.
- Do not cite a paper for a claim it does not make.
- Mark missing citations as `[citation needed]`.

## Results Style

- Report baselines, metrics, and settings.
- Include negative or mixed results when relevant.
- Do not hide variance, failed seeds, or missing runs.
- Separate numerical result reporting from interpretation.

## Limitations Style

Limitations should be explicit and useful. Name the scope boundary and its consequence for the claim.

## Revision Style

When revising, preserve the evidence trail. If a claim changes, update the related brief, interpretation, and draft files.
